How to run a pickleball round robin

Updated 5 October 2026

A round robin is the standard way to run a pickleball social: everybody plays everybody, with no one eliminated. The name is used loosely, though. Some groups mean fixed teams that each meet every other team; others mean rotating partners, so each person plays with and against everyone. Both work, and they need different planning.

This guide covers both versions, how to choose courts and rounds, and how to handle byes and tie-breaks.

Two kinds of round robin

1. Fixed partners

Teams stay together. Every team plays every other team once. With 6 teams that's 5 rounds. This is the simplest to organise and the best choice for a league or club ladder where people sign up in pairs. Singles work the same way: each player faces every other player once.

2. Rotating partners

This is the "mixer" style, the same idea as an Americano in padel. Every round you get a new partner and new opponents, and your own points accumulate. It suits open play where people arrive alone, and it spreads good players around evenly.

Choose fixed partners for competitive pairs and rotating partners for a social night.

Courts and rounds

  • Doubles needs four players per court. 8 players use 2 courts, 12 players use 3 courts and so on.
  • Fixed teams with N teams need N−1 rounds if N is even (every team plays every other team once). If N is odd, one team has a bye each round, so you play N rounds.
  • Rotating partners with 8 players can finish in 7 rounds, with everyone partnering everyone once.
  • Time: multiply the number of rounds by the time per round, and add a few minutes between rounds for rotating.

Two quick examples

Six teams, fixed partners, three courts: every team plays every other team once, which is 15 matches in total. With three courts that's 3 matches a round, so 5 rounds. Twelve players, rotating partners, three courts: nobody rests, and each round everyone has a new partner and new opponents. You can play as many rounds as you have time for.

Scoring

Standard pickleball games are played to 11 and won by two. That is fine for a league, but it makes round lengths unpredictable. Open-play organizers often use one of these instead:

  • A shorter race, for example to 9 or to 7, still win by two.
  • A fixed total. Play until the two scores add up to 16, 21 or 24, and each player keeps the points their side scored. This keeps rounds the same length and suits rotating partners.
  • A timer. Play until the clock ends, then record the score.

Whatever you choose, announce it before the first game.

Byes and odd numbers

If the numbers don't divide into foursomes, one or more people sit out each round. Make it fair:

  • rotate the byes so everyone sits out the same number of times;
  • don't give anyone two byes in a row if it can be avoided;
  • decide what a bye is worth in the standings (nothing, half a game's points, or the round's average).

Ranking the results

Most round robins rank by matches won, then point difference, then points scored. For rotating partners where everyone's own points count, rank by total points and use wins and difference to break ties. Write the order on a whiteboard so there are no arguments at the end.

Common mistakes

  • Mixing up fixed and rotating partners halfway through the night.
  • Not setting a game length, so one court runs ten minutes long.
  • Letting the same two players sit out repeatedly.
  • Waiting until the end to work out the table. Update it every round.

The same ideas apply to padel; see how to run a padel Americano for the rotating-partner version in more detail.

Doing it with Rallymix

Rallymix covers both kinds of round robin for pickleball.

  1. Tap New session, then choose Pickleball under What are you playing?.
  2. For rotating partners, choose the Americano format. For fixed partners, choose Team Americano (shown as Round robin · Fixed partners).
  3. Tap Next: players. For teams, enter both players of each pair in Who's playing? Add teams. Switch on Singles (one player per side) if each player is their own team.
  4. Tap Next: courts & scoring and set the number of Courts. Under Scoring choose Total points, First to (a race with win by two) or Timed.
  5. Use the suggested number of rounds, then tap Start session. Rests are shared out evenly and nobody sits out twice in a row when it can be avoided.
