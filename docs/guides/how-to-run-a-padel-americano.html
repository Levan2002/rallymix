How to run a padel Americano

Updated 5 October 2026

An Americano is the easiest way to turn a group of padel players into a proper evening. Nobody needs a regular partner, everybody plays with everybody, and there's a winner at the end. It's also easy to run badly: if one person plays six matches and another plays four, or the same two players keep ending up together, people notice.

This guide explains how an Americano works and how to run one so it feels fair, whether you do it with paper, a spreadsheet or an app.

What an Americano is

An Americano is a social tournament format for doubles. Every round you play with a different partner against a different pair. Instead of winning a match, you win points, and every point your pair scores goes onto your own tally. After the last round, the player with the most points wins.

That's why it works so well for mixed-level groups: a strong player and a beginner share a court, and both still have something to play for.

What you need

  • Players in multiples of four if you can. One court takes four players, so 8, 12 or 16 players fill 2, 3 or 4 courts with nobody waiting. Other numbers work too, with rests (see below).
  • As many courts as players ÷ 4. Fewer courts means more people resting each round.
  • A fixed total for every match. The usual choices are 16, 21, 24 or 32 points. Pick one number and use it all night.
  • Somebody to keep the schedule. That's the part most organizers dislike, because the pairings have to be worked out in advance.

How the scoring works

Say you play to 24 points in total. You don't stop when someone reaches 13; you play until the two sides' scores add up to 24. If your pair wins 15–9, each of you scores 15 and the other pair scores 9 each. A fixed total keeps every match about the same length and means no one needs a tie-break at 12–12 (both sides simply get 12).

The alternative is a race: first to 11 or 21, win by two. Totals are more predictable for scheduling, which is why most Americano nights use them.

How many rounds should you play?

The classic answer for 8 players is 7 rounds. Each player has seven possible partners, so in 7 rounds everyone partners everyone exactly once. For bigger groups, don't chase a perfect schedule. Decide how long you have, divide by the time per round, and check the result with the rest maths below.

A quick time estimate: rounds × minutes per round, plus a few minutes between rounds. Seven rounds at 15 minutes is just under two hours. A 16-point match is quicker than a 32-point one.

Odd numbers and who sits out

If your group isn't a multiple of four, someone rests every round. That's where most grumbling starts, so be deliberate:

  • Share the rests evenly. With 9 players and 2 courts, one person sits out each round, so 9 rounds means everyone rests exactly once. With 10 players and 2 courts, two sit out each round, so 5 rounds gives everyone one rest.
  • Don't let anyone sit out twice in a row while others haven't rested at all.
  • Decide in advance what resting is worth. Options are nothing, half the points of a match, or the average of the round. Without a rule, the person who rested early is simply behind on points.

Tie-breaks

Agree them before the first ball is hit. A sensible order is points won, then matches won, then point difference. When you rank by points, exact ties are fairly rare, so the order mostly matters for the places that count: the podium.

Common mistakes

  • Making up pairings on the spot. You will repeat partners and forget who has rested.
  • Letting matches run over. Without a round timer, one slow court delays everyone. Ring a bell or use a countdown.
  • Changing the rules mid-night. If you switch from 24 to 16 points halfway, the totals stop being comparable.
  • Not planning for late arrivals and early leavers. Know in advance whether a late player joins the next round and how their missed matches are handled.
  • Forgetting to share the results. The table is half the fun. Post it to the group chat.

Doing it with Rallymix

Rallymix does the scheduling for you. Here's an Americano from start to finish.

  1. On the home screen, tap New session.
  2. Step 1 of 3, What are you playing?: choose Padel, then the Americano format card.
  3. Tap Next: players. In Who's playing?, type the names, tap the suggestions under Your players, or paste a list of names from your group chat, one per line.
  4. Tap Next: courts & scoring. Set Courts, then choose Total points under Scoring and the number you play to (for example 24). Rallymix shows a suggested number of rounds; tap Use the suggested rounds or set your own.
  5. Under Ranking, Rank by is set to points won for a classic Americano. If you have odd numbers, choose what sitting out earns under Points for sitting out.
  6. Tap Start session. The Round tab shows each court and who is resting. Open a match, tap one side's points and the other side fills in, then tap Save score.
  7. When the round is done, tap Go to round 2 and carry on. The Table tab is the live leaderboard, and the share button sends the current round or the table to your group chat.
  8. If someone leaves or arrives late, open the Players tab: switch them off, or add the late arrival. Only rounds that haven't started are re-planned.
  9. After the last round, tap See the final table and Share the results.
