Americano vs Mexicano in padel

Updated 5 October 2026

If you play social padel, you've heard both words: "We're doing an Americano on Thursday" and "Saturday is a Mexicano." They look similar on the sheet, but they feel different on court. The short version: in an Americano the schedule decides who plays together; in a Mexicano the leaderboard does.

This guide explains both, so you can pick the right one for your group.

The Americano in one paragraph

All the pairings are planned in advance. Every round you get a new partner and new opponents, and your own points add up over the night. A well-made Americano gives every player the same number of matches and spreads partners and opponents evenly. With 8 players, 7 rounds is enough for everyone to partner everyone once. Because the schedule ignores the standings, a strong player can end up with a weak partner against another strong player, and that's by design.

The Mexicano in one paragraph

The first round is drawn at random. From round two on, the standings decide the courts. The top four players play together on the first court, the next four on the second, and so on. Within each court the partners are set by rank, usually either 1st and 3rd against 2nd and 4th, or 1st and 4th against 2nd and 3rd. The result: after a round or two, people play others close to their own level, and games get tighter.

A Mexicano round, step by step

Take eight players on two courts. Round one is drawn at random and you play it to your usual total. Now the table has a first to eighth place. Court one takes the players ranked 1 to 4 and court two takes 5 to 8. If your group uses 1st & 4th against 2nd & 3rd, the leader partners the player in fourth place on court one. Scores go in, the table updates and round three is drawn from the new ranking. Winners drift toward court one, losers drift down, and after a few rounds most matches are close.

Which should you choose?

Pick an Americano when…

  • the group mixes levels and you want everyone to play with everyone;
  • it's a club night and some players are new;
  • you want to publish the whole schedule before the first ball.

Pick a Mexicano when…

  • the group is of a similar level and wants close games;
  • you care about a proper competitive ranking;
  • you are happy to draw each round after the previous one ends.

A middle path

Many groups run an Americano for the first few rounds and a Mexicano for the final rounds, or simply alternate week to week. Whichever you choose, set the scoring before you start: a fixed total of points per match (16, 21, 24 or 32) keeps both formats predictable.

What both formats need

  • A fixed number of points per match, or a fixed time per round.
  • A rule for resting players when the numbers don't divide by four. Everyone should rest about the same number of times.
  • A tie-break order you agreed up front.
  • Somebody keeping the table up to date, because in a Mexicano the next round can't be drawn until all scores are in.

That last point is the Mexicano's one practical drawback. If one court plays slowly, everyone waits. A round timer helps, and so does a rule that unfinished matches are scored where they stand when the timer rings.

Doing it with Rallymix

Rallymix has both formats, and the setup is nearly identical. For a Mexicano:

  1. Tap New session on the home screen.
  2. In What are you playing? choose Padel, then the Mexicano format card. Its card reads: Round 1 is random, then the leaderboard sets the courts so games stay close.
  3. Tap Next: players, add the players, then tap Next: courts & scoring. Pick the number of courts, your scoring and the rounds. Under Ranking, Court pairing lets you choose between 1st & 3rd vs 2nd & 4th and 1st & 4th vs 2nd & 3rd.
  4. Tap Start session. Round 1 is drawn for you. Enter each score with Save score.
  5. When every match in the round is saved, tap Draw round 2. The courts are set by the table: the top four play on the first court.
  6. Check the Table tab at any point, and use the share button to send it to the group chat.

To compare, start an Americano instead: choose the Americano card in step 2 and the whole schedule is planned for you before round 1. Both formats are free for up to 8 players on 2 courts.
